# ADR-0022: Signal advisory is the only score - person scoring removed

- Status: accepted - owner sign-off 2026-06-13
- Date: 2026-06-12
- Supersedes: ADR-0019 (in part - the two person-scoring triggers [approval promotion + on-demand re-score], the `scorings.provenance` convention, and qualification-as-a-read; its drafting-stage removal, synchronous on-demand generation, enrichment relocation, and manual-no-auto-score decisions all stand), ADR-0005 (in part - the mandated per-person Scoring and the outcome-binds-to-score learning model; the one-to-many signal fan-out shape stands), ADR-0020 (in part - its "qualification leaves status" decision ends in removal rather than a derived read; the configurable-pipeline model stands in full), ADR-0007 (in part - its "the score gate decides what is eligible for enrichment; enrichment is never applied to below-bar prospects" restriction loses its referent and is withdrawn; enrichment-is-user-triggered-and-optional stands in full)
- Refines: ADR-0017 (the type-keyed advisory rubrics stand and become the only rubric consumer; their no-learning-loop-pollution purpose is now satisfied trivially - there is no durable per-person score for the cheap pass to pollute)
- Source: openspec/changes/adr-signal-only-scoring/system-design.md (Decisions and trade-offs); owner directive 2026-06-12; companion code change openspec/changes/remove-person-scoring/

## Context

ADR-0019 settled person scoring as two triggers: signal approval promotes the advisory hint into an `advisory`-provenance `scorings` row, and a synchronous re-score writes a fresh `llm`-provenance row; qualification (qualified / below_bar / unassessed) is a derived read over the latest buyer-rubric row (ADR-0019/0020). <!-- v:fact docs/adr/0019-generation-and-scoring-on-demand.md --> The owner now rejects the premise and we adopt the framing as a design position: the advisory score's job ends at informing the approve/dismiss verdict, and the human verdict is defined to be the qualification - so a person-keyed score maintained after approval restates a decision already made and must not exist (falsifiable as: no surface or table may present or store a fit verdict for a Person). <!-- v:decision --> The machinery the restatement carries is substantial: the `scorings` table with a provenance convention and sentinel columns, an LLM scorer with a versioned prompt, the qualification read with its rubric-kind and tie-break rules, learning-loop exclusion invariants spanning two tables, and score/qualification surface on every People view. <!-- v:fact src/lib/qualify, src/lib/db/schema.ts scorings --> Meanwhile the `outcomes.score_at_time` snapshot that justified durable per-person scores (D7) has no writer - the route that wrote outcomes was deleted in the engagement rework - so the learning loop the scores were kept for is schema-only today. <!-- v:fact src (no outcome write path; outcomes referenced only in schema.ts and the prototype) -->

## Decision

We will make the signal the only scored thing in the system and remove person scoring entirely. <!-- v:decision -->

- **`signal_advisory` is the single scoring concept** (ADR-0017's shape, unchanged): one row per signal, scored by the advisory filter against the active rubric of the signal's kind, shown at triage where the judgment happens. <!-- v:derives ADR-0017 -->
- **The `scorings` entity is dropped** - the table, the `advisory | llm` provenance convention, the sentinel provider/prompt/model values, and both write paths (the approval promotion and the on-demand re-score). Approval's transaction shrinks to the SignalDecision plus the routed entity. The LLM scorer core and the `icp_score` prompt survive as the advisory filter's internals (relocated into the triage slice - the advisory filter is their only consumer). <!-- v:decision -->
- **Qualification is removed, not re-derived**: no qualified / below_bar / unassessed read exists. People surfaces carry identity, pipeline position, source, and artifact facets. "Why is this person here" is answered by the originating signal's advisory score, one join away via `person.signal_id`; a manual-origin person has no score anywhere, accepted by design (the alternative - an advisory-derived badge - would pin every manual person at a dead `unassessed` with no path out, since no re-score exists). <!-- v:decision -->
- **The enrichment eligibility gate goes with qualification**: ADR-0007's "score gate decides enrichment eligibility; never enrich below-bar" restriction is withdrawn - the human's triage approval is the gate, so any admitted person (or manual entry) is enrichable on an explicit click. Enrichment stays user-triggered and optional (ADR-0007's core); what changes is that the advisory score no longer acts as a second, automatic spend filter behind the human's verdict. <!-- v:decision -->
- **`outcomes.score_at_time` is dropped** in the same migration, and the D7 learning loop remains deferred - with its data forfeit named honestly: today's `signal_advisory` is a mutable, version-unpinned hint (one row per signal, refreshed in place by the job, recording `rubric_kind` but no rubric version or provenance) and is NOT learning-loop substrate. Dropping `scorings` forfeits the system's only rubric-version anchor; when D7 is designed it must introduce its own append-only, rubric-version-pinned, provenance-carrying record (or make the advisory snapshot-at-decision durable) - that is the accepted cost of removing person scoring now. Repointing `score_at_time` today was rejected as designing the binding ahead of the loop. <!-- v:decision -->
- **Rubrics stay as-is** (config-as-data, kinds `icp | peer | company`, one active per kind, additive versions); their sole consumer is the advisory filter. <!-- v:derives ADR-0017 -->
- The removal ships as the companion `spec-driven` change `remove-person-scoring` (one destructive forward-only migration; pre-launch dev data, no preservation). <!-- v:fact openspec/changes/remove-person-scoring/proposal.md -->

## Consequences

Easier: exactly one LLM scoring pass exists (the per-signal advisory filter), so no code path can spend LLM budget scoring a person - completing the cost-control trajectory ADR-0019 started. <!-- v:derives ADR-0019 --> One score concept in one place kills a whole class of consistency rules: the provenance exclusion, the rubric-kind-filtered latest-row read with its tie-break, and the outcome-provenance carryover all become unnecessary instead of enforced. <!-- v:decision --> The People read-model loses a join; approval loses its only conditional write. Harder/accepted: no per-person fit number survives approval - if practice shows the People list needs a fit cue, surfacing the origin signal's advisory score through the existing join is additive at the schema level, but the cue is rubric-kind-polysemic (buyer-fit only for `icp`-kind signals; peer- and company-kind advisories measure different things) and absent for manual origin - any such surface must branch on `signal_advisory.rubric_kind`, never render `score` uniformly. <!-- v:decision --> The learning loop loses its designed binding and cannot be built over today's `signal_advisory` as-is (see the Decision's named forfeit); its design - including the durable record it needs and how a manual person without a signal participates, if at all - is an open question this ADR deliberately does not answer. <!-- v:decision --> With `scorings` removed, no row references a rubric version, so ADR-0017's "a Rubric is immutable once any Scoring references it" invariant is unanchored: rubric versioning survives only as config history until a future learning-grade record re-establishes a version pin. <!-- v:decision --> Historical dev-stage scores are destroyed with the table; the data is disposable pre-launch dev data, so "rollback" means restoring a dev snapshot if one exists, else re-seeding - there is no down-migration. <!-- v:decision -->

Rules out: any future feature that stores a score, rating, or fit verdict on a Person row or a person-keyed table - such a feature must either read the origin signal's advisory or supersede this ADR. <!-- v:decision --> The supersessions are partial and named in the header: ADR-0019's on-demand generation model, ADR-0020's pipelines, ADR-0005's fan-out shape, ADR-0007's user-triggered-optional enrichment core, and ADR-0017's advisory rubrics all remain in force. For ADR-0017 the chain is two hops: its "only the qualify step persists a Scoring" mechanism was already withdrawn by ADR-0019; what this ADR refines is the remaining-in-force rubric shape (type-keyed kinds, one active per kind) and its now-sole consumer role. <!-- v:derives header Supersedes/Refines -->
